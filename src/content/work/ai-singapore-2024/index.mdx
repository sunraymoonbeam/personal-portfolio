## The problem

Type "a tired man walks up a hill" and get a 3D character actually doing it.
Animation is expensive and slow to produce by hand, so the prize is obvious.

The model architecture was the part everyone wanted to work on. It was not the
part that was blocking us.

## Most of the year went into data

Motion capture archives are long, inconsistently rigged, barely labelled, and
full of takes where someone walks to their mark and stands still for nine
seconds. Before any of it can train a model it has to be cleaned, retargeted
onto one skeleton, segmented into meaningful motions, and described in words.

I built that pipeline, and processed about **1,100 hours** through it. It is not
a glamorous sentence to write. It was the whole project.

## What I built on top

A diffusion model over motion sequences in PyTorch, conditioned on text, plus
experiments using multimodal video LLMs to generate captions for clips nobody
had labelled — bootstrapping the descriptions we were missing.

## What it taught me

Two things. First, that the bottleneck is almost never the model. Second, that
being the person willing to fix the data is a genuinely useful thing to be,
even though nobody puts it in the demo.
