## What we were trying to make easier

Animating a game character usually involves designing a character, rigging it and building each movement by hand. At AI Singapore, I worked on a GenAI MVP for a gaming company that would turn a text prompt into 3D motion. A prompt such as “a man walking” could produce motion that a developer could bring into Unity.

## Making the data usable

The motion came from different sources and in different formats. It included joint positions, movement relationships and velocities. I worked on a modular pipeline to bring those datasets into one training format. That preparation mattered as much as the diffusion model we trained with PyTorch.

The model also needed motion clips paired with descriptions. Some clips had missing or incomplete prompts, so we used a video captioning model to generate descriptions in the data pipeline. The resulting work improved our text-to-motion metrics by 20% over baseline, as recorded in my résumé.

## Beyond the model

I helped build the training and deployment pipeline and improved the GitLab CI/CD checks. Testing and deployment time fell by 80%. I also made onboarding materials and a small interactive repository for practising Linux and version control. Explaining those basics to teammates with different backgrounds made me a clearer engineer.

This project taught me to work from the data backwards. A more capable model cannot compensate for inconsistent motion files and missing descriptions.
