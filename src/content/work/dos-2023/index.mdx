## The ask

The department publishes a lot of tables. Internally, people spent real time
answering questions that were technically already public — "what was the median
household income in 2019" — because finding the right table is harder than
reading it.

This was mid-2023. The obvious thing was to point an LLM at it.

## What I built

A LangChain pipeline over GPT-3.5 that took a plain-English question, worked out
which table could answer it, pulled the relevant rows, and answered with a
citation back to the source table.

## The part that nearly sank it

It was **confidently wrong**. Not often, but enough. And a confidently wrong
number attached to an official statistic is far worse than no answer, because
someone will repeat it in a meeting.

So I spent the second half of the internship on the unglamorous half:

- Wrote **200 evaluation questions** with the analysts, including ones we knew
  the system should refuse.
- Measured it honestly. It got **64%** right.
- Added an explicit "I am not confident, here is the table, check it yourself"
  path, and made that the default whenever retrieval was ambiguous.

## What I took away

That a demo and a system are different things, and the gap between them is
almost entirely measurement. I did not know the word "eval" at the time. I was
building one anyway, badly, because there was no other way to know if it worked.

Everything I do now with tracing and evaluation started here.
