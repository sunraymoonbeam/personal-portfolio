## Why bother

I wanted to know how much of "sounding like a person" is style rather than
substance. Turns out a surprising amount of it is: sentence length, how often
you swear, whether you send three short messages or one long one, and what you
say when you have nothing to say.

## What I built

An export pipeline that pulls chat history out of Telegram and WhatsApp,
strips anything identifying about the other person, and turns it into
instruction pairs. Then a LoRA fine-tune of LLaMA 3 8B with Unsloth, and the
same thing on Gemma 3 for comparison. The winner gets served behind FastAPI
with the weights on S3.

## The part that actually mattered

Not the model. **The data cleaning.** My first run learned to reply "ok" to
everything, because that is genuinely what I do most. I had to downsample the
boring turns and keep the ones where I said something, which meant writing a
crude "is this message interesting" classifier first.

## Why LoRA and not a full fine-tune

I have one GPU. A full fine-tune of an 8B model does not fit, and renting one
for an experiment about my own texting habits felt like a bad use of money. LoRA
trains a small set of adapter weights instead, which fits in 16GB and takes
about six hours.

## How I tested it

I put twenty real messages and twenty generated ones in a list and asked eleven
friends to mark which were mine. They got **48% right**, which is worse than
guessing. That is the whole result, and I like that it is a number rather than
a feeling.

## What I would not do again

Train on messages from group chats. The model picked up other people's habits
and started using phrases I have never said in my life.
