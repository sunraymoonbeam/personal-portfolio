## The experiment

Echo is a personal project: I wanted to see what a model trained on my own
messages could sound like. I used five years of Telegram data to build a
digital clone of my writing style. It is an experiment in personalisation, not
a claim that a model can stand in for a person.

It works, which is the funny part and the unsettling part at the same time.

<Figure src={chat} alt="A chat with the fine-tuned model, which replies in Singlish about wanting to eat Kovan food." caption="It picked up the Singlish, the LOLs and the habit of suggesting food. I did not tell it to." />

## Five years of messages is not a dataset

A chat export is a pile of JSON with no notion of a turn, a topic or a reply.
Most of the work was turning it into something a model could learn from:
deciding what counts as a conversation, who said what, which messages to keep
and how to pair a prompt with a response.

<Figure src={dataprep} alt="The data preparation screen, showing statistics about the processed chat export." caption="The unglamorous half, and the half that decided how good the result was." />

## How I built it

I fine-tuned open-source LLaMA 3 and Gemma 3 models with Unsloth. Its
memory-efficient training path made the experiments practical on the hardware
available to me, which was a single T4.

I split the application into small services: one for data preparation, one for
training and inference, and a Streamlit front end over both. Everything runs in
Docker on one machine.

<Figure src={architecture} alt="Architecture diagram: a Streamlit frontend, a data preparation service and an Unsloth training backend, all in Docker on one EC2 instance with a Tesla T4." caption="Three containers on one GPU box. Not elegant, but every part of it was mine to debug." />

## What I learned

Most of the work turned out to be outside the model itself: cleaning the data,
shaping the training set and deciding how to serve the result. It also made me
think more carefully about what it means to build a personal system from
private conversation data.

The data has its own limits. A chat export reflects who I was speaking to and
what conversations I kept, so the model can only learn from that sample.
