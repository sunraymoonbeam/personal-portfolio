## Why

I was paying about $180 a month for managed versions of things I could run
myself, and I was learning nothing from any of it. The bill was annoying. The
not-learning was worse.

## What runs on it

Two boxes, one at Hetzner and one at Netcup. **Dokploy** handles deploys,
**Traefik** does routing and certificates, **n8n** runs the automations, and
everything sits behind Tailscale so nothing but the web ports are public.

On top of that there are eight Telegram agents. Each one owns exactly one job:
log a expense, sync a note, take a backup, or read the marine fuel assessments
every morning and tell me what moved.

## The part that took longest

Not the setup — the **failure modes**. A homelab that silently stops working is
worse than no homelab, because you trust it. Every agent now reports to a
health channel, and anything that has not checked in for an hour pages me.

## What it costs

About **$26 a month**, down from roughly $180. The honest caveat is that my own
time is not in that number, and the first month cost me most of a weekend.

## What I would tell someone starting one

Put the monitoring in before the fun parts. I did it the other way round and
spent a fortnight not noticing a backup job had been failing since day three.
