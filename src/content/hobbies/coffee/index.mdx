For most of my life, coffee meant kopi. Then I got curious and bought a V60.
My first cup tasted like blueberries. I didn't know coffee could taste like
blueberries.

Since then, what should have been a simple morning drink has turned into a
small daily experiment. It's also a ritual, and a big part of why I look
forward to waking up.

The fun part is how many things change the cup. I tweak one, taste what
changes, then try to work out why it changed. These are the ones I play with:

- **The beans.** Where they grew and how they were processed decide what the
  cup can taste like at all.
  - **Washed:** the fruit comes off before the beans dry, so the cup tastes
    clean and bright, with clear acidity.
  - **Natural:** the beans dry inside the whole cherry, which gives fruity,
    heavier and sometimes funky flavours.
  - **Honey:** some of the sticky fruit stays on while the beans dry, so the
    cup sits in between: sweet, with a rounder body.
- **The roast.** Light roasts keep more of the bean's own flavours, but
  they're denser and harder to extract. Dark roasts taste more of the roast
  and turn bitter if you push them. Beans taste best a week or two after
  roasting.
- **Grind size.** Finer grounds extract more and slow the water down. Too fine
  tastes bitter and dry; too coarse tastes sour and thin.
- **Coffee-to-water ratio.** This sets the strength. About 1 g of coffee to
  16 g of water is a common place to start.
- **Water temperature.** Hotter water extracts more. Most recipes sit between
  90 and 96°C: hotter for light roasts, cooler for dark ones.
- **The water itself.** Minerals carry the flavour out. Very soft water tastes
  flat, and very hard water tastes chalky.
- **The brewing method.** A pour-over like the V60 runs water through the
  coffee and a paper filter, for a clean, light cup. Immersion, like the
  AeroPress, steeps everything together, for a heavier body.

Since the V60 I've tried the AeroPress, immersion brewing, cold brew and a few
other ways of making filter coffee. Espresso would be the next rabbit hole.

I've tried plenty of other recipes too: coffee mixed with tea (with black
oolong, it's amazing), dirty matcha, which is a matcha latte with a shot of
espresso, and my infamous Ferrero Rocher cold brew.

In my spare time I also love drinking coffee around the world, from specialty
cafes to chai in the hills of northern India.
