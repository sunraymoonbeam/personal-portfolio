For most of my life, coffee meant kopi. Then I started working full time, and
caffeine became less optional.

I bought a V60, and what should have been a simple morning drink turned into a
small daily experiment. In theory it's coffee and water in, coffee out. In
practice there's a lot to get right:

| Factor | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| The beans and roast | Light roasts are denser and harder to extract, so they need hotter water and a finer grind. Dark roasts give up their flavour fast and turn bitter if pushed. Beans taste best a week or two after roasting. |
| Grind size | Finer grounds extract more and slow the water down. Too fine tastes bitter and dry; too coarse tastes sour and thin. |
| Coffee-to-water ratio | Sets the strength. About 1 g of coffee to 16 g of water is a common starting point. |
| Water temperature | Hotter water extracts more. Most recipes sit between 90 and 96°C: the top of that for light roasts, the bottom for dark. |
| The water itself | Minerals in the water carry the flavour out. Very soft water tastes flat, and very hard water tastes chalky. |
| Brew time | Mostly the result of the grind and the pour. It's the easiest check that something has changed. |

Change any one and the same beans taste different.

of making filter coffee. Espresso is the next rabbit hole.
