My introduction to guitar was slightly backwards. I started on a guitarrón.

More importantly, I started on a fretless instrument.

Most guitars give you very obvious boundaries. Put your finger behind this fret
and you get this note. The neck is divided neatly into little boxes showing you
where everything belongs.

Take the frets away and things become considerably less forgiving.

There is no metal line guaranteeing that your finger is in the right place.
Move slightly in one direction and the note changes. You have to listen more
carefully and learn where the sound actually lives, rather than relying
entirely on where you think your finger should go.

I like that idea beyond the instrument too.

A lot of things become easier once somebody draws lines around them and tells
you where the correct positions are. But those lines can also become something
you stop questioning.

A fretless instrument gives you a little more freedom. It also gives you many
more opportunities to sound terrible.

These days I mostly play acoustic guitar, which is considerably more
conventional. I am also not particularly good.

That is probably part of why I still enjoy it. There is no real objective other
than picking it up, learning something new and eventually making the noise
coming out of it sound a little closer to the noise I had in my head.
