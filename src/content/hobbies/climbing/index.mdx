I started climbing in 2018 through a PE elective in school. I had never really
thought about climbing before that, but the first time I got on the wall,
something about it felt strangely natural.

<Figure src={gym} alt="A bouldering gym, mid-session, with a wall of coloured holds." caption="Where it started. Still where most of it happens." />

Two years later, I broke both my arms and had to stop climbing for a while.
Coming back took time, and I had to rebuild both strength and confidence.

Since then, climbing has taken me on a lot of adventures and introduced me to
some of my closest friends. I have climbed in Malaysia, Thailand and Hampi in
India, and every place has made the sport feel slightly different.

<Photos cols={2} ratio="portrait" label="Same sport, different rock">

  <Shot src={roof} alt="Bouldering upside down under a granite roof, feet above head." caption="Hampi, India. This is what the granite does to you." />

What keeps me coming back is the problem solving.

A climb can feel completely impossible until one tiny thing changes. Turn a hip
slightly. Move a foot a few centimetres. Grip the same hold from a different
angle. Suddenly a move that made no sense works.

I like that about climbing. Progress is not always about trying harder.
Sometimes it is about noticing the one small thing that changes everything.
