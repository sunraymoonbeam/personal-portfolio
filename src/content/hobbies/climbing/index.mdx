I started climbing in 2018 through a PE elective in school. I'd never really
thought about it before, but the first time I got on the wall, something about
it felt strangely natural.

<Photos>
  <Shot src={gym} alt="A bouldering gym, mid-session, with a wall of coloured holds." />
</Photos>

Two years later I broke both my arms and had to stop for a while. Coming back
took time: I had to rebuild my strength and my confidence.

<Photos>
  <Shot src={plate} alt="X-ray of a forearm with a metal plate and screws along the bone." caption="I can sing Titanium with a straight face now." />
  <Shot src={healed} alt="X-ray of a healed forearm with no hardware." />
</Photos>

Since then climbing has taken me to Batu Caves in Kuala Lumpur, Thailand and
Hampi in India, and
introduced me to some of my closest friends. Every place has made the sport
feel slightly different.

<Photos>
  <Shot src={limestone} alt="Climbing a steep limestone face on a rope." caption="Batu Caves, Kuala Lumpur." />
  <Shot src={roof} alt="Bouldering upside down under a granite roof, feet above head." caption="Hampi, India." />
</Photos>

What keeps me coming back is the problem solving. A move can feel impossible
for a whole session, and then I turn my hip slightly, move a foot a few
centimetres or grip the same hold from a different angle, and it suddenly goes.
Trying harder rarely fixes it.

I think a lot of life works the same way. Something small, changed in the right
place, can change everything.
