I started climbing in 2018 through a PE elective in school. I'd never really
thought about climbing before, but the first time I got on the wall, it felt
strangely natural.

<Figure src={gym} alt="A bouldering gym, mid-session, with a wall of coloured holds." caption="Where it started, and still where most of it happens." />

Two years later I broke both my arms and had to stop for a while. Coming back
took time: I had to rebuild my strength and my confidence.

<Photos cols={2} ratio="portrait">
  <Shot src={plate} alt="X-ray of a forearm with a metal plate and screws along the bone." caption="I can sing Titanium with a straight face now." />
  <Shot src={healed} alt="X-ray of a healed forearm with no hardware." />

Since then climbing has taken me to Malaysia, Thailand and Hampi in India, and
introduced me to some of my closest friends. Limestone, granite and gym plastic
each climb a little differently, so every trip teaches me something new.

<Photos cols={2} ratio="portrait">
  <Shot src={limestone} alt="Climbing a steep limestone face on a rope." />
  <Shot src={roof} alt="Bouldering upside down under a granite roof, feet above head." caption="Hampi, India." />
</Photos>

What keeps me coming back is the problem solving. A move can feel impossible
for a whole session, then work the moment I turn my hip a little, move a foot a
few centimetres higher or grip the same hold from a different angle.
