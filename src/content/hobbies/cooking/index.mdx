Food is one of the few things you can enjoy from another culture without
knowing anything else about it. When I was growing up in Singapore, that's exactly why
Western food appealed to me. It was different from what I ate every day, so
it's what I learned to cook first.

Getting older did the opposite. I started appreciating the food I grew up with,
and these days I cook a lot of it for my family: char kway teow, Hokkien mee,
prawn mee, chicken rice.

  <Shot src={mise} alt="A kitchen counter covered in bowls of prepared prawns, pork and chicken beside sauces and a knife block." caption="The part nobody photographs." />

Chicken rice is my favourite because it looks simple and isn't. The rice needs
aromatics and chicken fat, so first you render the fat. The stock has to carry
the whole plate. The chicken has to cook through while the skin stays soft and
gelatinous, and then you have to cut it cleanly enough that it still looks like
food when it reaches the table. That's a lot of decisions for a plate that
disappears in ten minutes.

Cooking local food is different from trying something new. I'm not inventing
anything. I'm trying to get a taste I already know exactly right, and when I
do, there's something very comforting about it. It tastes like home.
