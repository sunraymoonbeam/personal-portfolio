These days I cook a lot for my family, mostly the food I grew up with: char
kway teow, Hokkien mee, prawn mee and chicken rice.

It didn't start that way. Growing up in Singapore, Western food seemed more
exciting because it was different, so that's what I learned to cook first.

<Figure src={clams} alt="Linguine in a pot, clams in another, and garlic softening in a pan." caption="The Western food era. I stand by the clams." />

Getting older pulled me back to local food. Part of the appeal is that I
already know exactly how it should taste, so I can tell when I've got it wrong.

<Figure src={mise} alt="A kitchen counter covered in bowls of prepared prawns, pork and chicken beside sauces and a knife block." caption="The part nobody photographs, and the part that decides how dinner goes." />

Chicken rice is my favourite to make because it looks simple and isn't. The
rice needs aromatics and chicken fat, so first you render the fat. The stock
has to carry the whole plate. The chicken has to cook through while the skin
stays soft and gelatinous, and then you have to cut it cleanly enough that it
still looks like food when it reaches the table. That's a lot of decisions for
a plate that disappears in ten minutes.
