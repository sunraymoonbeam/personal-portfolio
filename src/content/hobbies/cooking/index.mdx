Food is one of the few things you can enjoy from another culture without
knowing anything else about it. Music is another: a song can move you in a
language you don't speak. Dance says what words can't, and you can stand in
front of a painting without a guidebook. But food is the one you can carry
home in your memory and try to make again in your own kitchen.

When I was growing up in Singapore, Western food really appealed to me. It was
different from what I ate every day, and if I'm honest, I thought Western
meant better. So it's what I learned to cook first, and a good vongole is
still always nice.

Getting older, I started to appreciate the food I grew up with. These days I
especially love experimenting with hawker dishes and trying to get them right
at home: char kway teow, Hokkien mee, prawn mee, chicken rice.

Chicken rice is my favourite because it looks simple and isn't. The rice alone
takes a lot of care:

1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, so it doesn't turn sticky.
2. Render the chicken fat, then fry minced garlic and ginger in it until
   they're fragrant but not brown.
3. Toast the rice in the fat and aromatics until the grains turn translucent.
4. Cook it in the stock from poaching the chicken, with a knotted pandan leaf.
5. Let it rest before you fluff it, so every grain stays separate.

Then the chicken has to cook through while the skin stays soft and
gelatinous, and you have to cut it cleanly enough that it still looks good on
the plate.

There's something really nice about pouring your love and effort into a dish
for the people you love. It might not be restaurant quality, but you can taste
the effort and the warmth in it, the same way I always could in my mum's
cooking.
